At Locus Robotics, I worked as a Product Quality intern with a focus on the second generation of 
<a href="https://locusrobotics.com/locusone/fleet/locus-origin-collaborative-robot">Origin bots</a>, a collaborative robot
that increases pick efficiency for order fulfillment. I worked on several projects throughout the summer that spanned from 
early manufacturing, to sustaining engineering, to end-of-life testing. Locus operates on a RAAS (Robots-as-a-Service) model,
meaning that bots were frequently returned or introduced based on peak warehouse needs. Within their lifespan, robots worked at multiple sites,
with repairs and maintenance in between before eventually being scrapped for components. 

My summer included 3 major projects. 
1. Determining the effectiveness of proactive failure monitoring in the field and in-house refurbishing for bot batteries. Essentially,
I connected Data Science monitoring data and predictions to internal and manufacturer testing, 
assessing how well we proactively identified battery failure types on site compared to manufacturer repair results.
2. Developing work instructions for internal rework processes, contract manufacturers, and component testing processes
3. Leading problem statement development, problem frequency characterizations across 90+ customer sites using robot error reporting and 
customer reported symptoms (Salesforce) for a major product escalation. This included a contract manufacturer site visit and an 8D presentation
to engineering, customer service, and company leadership.

Outside of my main projects, I assisted with material review board, NCMR processes, and gained a lot of hands-on exposure evaluating bot performance. 
I learned how to test and troubleshoot returned bots, PCBAs, LiDARs, and internal bot computers. I also performed PFMEAs for several assembly processes,
shadowing manufacturing. Working in quality gave me a great understanding of how warehouse robotics run and how a product gets to the market. Our team interacted with Customer Service, Product Management, Manufacturing, Engineering, Testing, and Data Science. I also learned how to communicate
 technical information to non-technical audiences.
